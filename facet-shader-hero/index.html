Facet

Light, cut into code.

A field of glass facets lit from wherever your pointer is. Move it, or don't — it drifts on its own too.

See the work
What's on screen is one WebGL draw call, no textures, no libraries. Each pixel:
  1. gets a centered, aspect-corrected coordinate, then the whole field is slowly rotated and drifted using u_time;
  2. is sorted into a triangular lattice by skewing the square pixel grid into 60° cells (the "facet" tiling);
  3. gets a fake surface normal per triangle, hashed from that triangle's cell id — this is what makes each facet catch light differently;
  4. is lit by a directional light aimed at u_mouse, plus a second light that orbits automatically via u_time so it's alive even with no input;
  5. gets a base hue keyed off the facet's hash, drifting slowly over time;
  6. is vignetted at the edges and dithered slightly to avoid color banding.
